### The fork, and why (B) wins on characterization rather than on size

Slice 2 recorded the choice as **(A)** widen the Plan-WI output contract in
`prompts/dual-plan-planner.template.md` — the sole consumer of
`{{HAT_QUESTIONS}}` — versus **(B)** state the obligation at the spine-authoring
tier. Both surfaces were characterized before choosing; (B) is ruled, and the
argument is that (A) is not a bigger version of the same change but a change to a
different artifact's job:

- **The planner brief's product is a PLAN, not a row.** Its contract is "exactly
  one markdown table, one row per proposed WI", columns `Plan-WI | Title | Covers
  | Interfaces | Predecessors`, with every perspective answered into `## Notes` as
  a Plan-WI or an explicit no-finding. There is no SR or LLR in its output for a
  `hat_refs` cell to attach to. A write instruction there is unfollowable by
  construction — the failure slice 2 predicted, confirmed by re-reading the
  template rather than assumed.
- **And that brief is the one artifact whose output must stay commensurable.**
  Two planners of different model families get byte-identical briefs and their
  tables are compared by a selection protocol; widening the contract to carry
  attributions would ship every adopter a schema change to the comparison itself,
  paid by every decomposition round, to serve a cell minted elsewhere.
- **(B)'s surface is already the cell's home.** `spine-authoring` §2(c) has named
  `Hat-Refs` since slice 1 — the RESYNC entry for the cell says in as many words
  that the skill "used to tell you to record a derived requirement's deriving hat
  as a prose label", and the cell replaced the label there. Putting the write
  rule anywhere else would mint a second home for one instruction, which is the
  duplication `PROCESS.md` §3 forbids.
- **It is also the surface the minting session reads.** `scope: kit`, fanned
  three ways (`.claude` / `.gemini` / `.agents`) with byte identity gated by
  `gen_skills_index --check-agents`, so the instruction reaches an adopter's
  agent by machinery that already exists.

Neither option needed the owner: (A) was declined on the brief's own stated
contract, and (B) changes no adopter contract — it states a judgement rule on the
surface that already carries the cell's other rules.

### What the writer teaches (the backfill's rule, not "fill the cell")

The point of the slice is that the backfill was trustworthy because of ONE stated
test, so the skill teaches the test. New `§2(c2)` at SR, and a new LLR bullet in
`§3`:

- **The `listens_for` test** — attribute a hat only where THAT hat's own
  `listens_for` names a failure THIS row prevents — stated together with the
  reading it displaces ("which lens could be held up to this row"), and with why
  that reading is fatal rather than merely loose: a roster whose hats are mostly
  `always` puts every name in every cell. It names the executable support:
  `hats.py list` prints each hat's `asks` and `listens_for`, `hats.py applicable`
  narrows to a context.
- **Empty is honest, with the two shapes that earn it** — a row naming a hat in
  order to REFUSE it as a basis (`SR-015`'s shape) and a row whose attribution's
  subject is gone (`SR-040`'s), generalized to the rule rather than cited as this
  repo's rows, since a marker naming this repo's records means nothing downstream.
- **Calibration** — one name is the common case; a cell heading for three is
  usually collecting failures that sibling rows state.
- **Coverage is warn-only in both directions and is not a quota** — a hat
  attributable to no row is evidence about the ROSTER (the `SAFETY` reading the
  backfill produced), not a hole to fill by attributing it somewhere.
- **LLR own refs only beyond parents** (`§3`) — the effective set is derived, so
  an own ref is earned only where the design row bears a hat no parent carries;
  copying down turns re-ruling one SR into a sweep over its children.
- **All four tiers enumerated** — `SR`/`LLR` in, `SN`/`TC` out, with the reason.
  That is the skill's own "a rule that names only some of its tiers" failure mode
  applied to the rule being written.

  would have to be re-stamped by every rewording. The existing pins do cover the
  surface: `gen_skills_index --check`/`--check-agents` (index freshness + the
  three-way byte identity) and `test_skills_index.py`.
- **`PROCESS.md` untouched** — it already states the cell, the derived-SR route
