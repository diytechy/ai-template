## Problem

Complex or optimization-shaped goals need deeper planning than a single-session
WI filing: the owner wants adversarial pressure on a proposed work-item
decomposition *before* build, without the hazardous iterate-until-agree loop —
the evidence shows peer consensus-seeking conforms rather than challenges
(sycophancy, consensus collapse) and rounds beyond 1–2 drift. Reviews merge
mechanically; rival decompositions (one planner proposes 4 WIs, another 3) do
not — so the reconciliation operation must be **select-and-port, never merge**
(plan fusion is unpublished; DAG union produces incoherent predecessors).

## Design (the protocol)

An **opt-in PROCESS_OPTIONS layer** ("dual-plan decomposition") on the existing
S8 chassis (fresh sessions, redacted prompts, verdict files, `agent_route`
heterogeneity, budgets). **No new engine.**

1. **Applies-when (selection at filing time, like `BuildTier`):** the goal
   declares an optimization target, the decomposition plausibly spans ≥2
   modules or any IF seam, or the scope is design-shaping (`strong`-tier). One
   declared trigger, not a vibe call. For a *measurable* optimization target,
   first preference stays a PB/NFR budget + `check_perf` (the harness is the
   adversary); the dual-plan round is for targets that resist measurement.
2. **Two independent planners,** different model families where available
   (`agent_route`; two samples of one family share blind spots), identical
   redacted briefs: the goal + the SR surface + the IF registry — never each
   other's output, never the driver's self-assessment.
3. **The commensurability contract:** each plan is a set of proposed WI rows
   where every WI cites the SR/goal clauses it covers and the IF-IDs it acts on
   (existing, or new-as-`Proposed` per the WI-191 rule). This is what makes
   rival plans mechanically comparable.
4. **Mechanical coverage pre-pass:** a trace-style check computes each plan's
   coverage of the goal clauses/SR surface, flags unresolvable IF refs, and
   emits the coverage diff — the objective feedback that published results say
   refinement needs (CRITIC's ablation; Huang et al.).
5. **One rubric-anchored cross-critique round** (hard cap n=1): each planner's
   plan is critiqued by the *other* family against a plan rubric seeded from
   the published decomposition criteria — solvability, completeness,
   non-redundancy — plus a seam-duplication anchor (WI-191's `B#`). One
   revision each. No further rounds.
6. **Arbiter select-and-port:** a fresh session, third family where available
   (never sharing a family with either planner unmitigated — self-preference
   bias), judging **artifacts, not conversations**: provenance-anonymized,
   position-swapped pairwise comparison against the rubric + the coverage
   report + the owner's original prompt, explicitly warned that more WIs ≠
   better (verbosity bias). It **selects one plan** and **ports named
   loser-WIs** that close coverage gaps, each port recorded as a cited delta in
   the verdict file.
7. **Acceptance:** the human `Attest` closes it per the gate philosophy; under
   `gate-policy: autonomous` the recorded-verdict rules govern as they do for
   the critique loop. The S8 "no LLM-judge tiebreaker between reviewer scores"
   ruling is untouched — this arbiter compares *plans*, not reviewer verdicts.

## Done-when

- [ ] The layer exists in PROCESS_OPTIONS with a crisp applies-when and the
      n=1 round cap stated as a rule, citing the knowledge pack.
- [ ] Planner/critic/arbiter prompt templates exist with redaction +
      anonymization + position-swap + anti-verbosity instructions embedded.
- [ ] The plan rubric exists with numbered anchors (G#/B#), including the
      seam-duplication anchor.
- [ ] The coverage diff is computable by a checker (or an explicitly recorded
      manual procedure if slice C is deferred — honest gap, not silence).
- [ ] One real goal has been run through the protocol end-to-end and the
      verdict + ports are recorded in the log.

## Non-goals

- No plan-merge engine, no unbounded rounds, no arbiter-mediated conversation
  between planners.
- No change to the review layer's "no debate rounds" ruling or the critique
  loop's contract — this is a sibling layer, not a rewrite.
- Not the default planning path: single-planner filing stays the norm; the
  layer fires only on its applies-when.
