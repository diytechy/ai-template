# WI-315 — Bind T1 task findability (the reworded anchor's mechanizable bar)

**WI:** WI-315 · **Status:** queued · **Delivers:** SR-054

## Problem

[`dashboard-usability.md`](../../rubrics/dashboard-usability.md) **T1** is the last
`SR-054` anchor whose mechanizable half has **no owner**. Under `SR-054` only
three children exist — `LLR-099`/`TC-102` (T2), `LLR-100`/`TC-103` (T3),
`LLR-105`/`TC-108` (T5). The [WI-300 ruling](./WI-300.2026-07-26.md)
called T1's core "assertable against the nav graph" and then bound everything
*except* it, leaving the same mechanizable-but-unbound hole the adversarial
review's F12 found for T6 (now `WI-314`). Every round, the critique re-judges by
eye a property a test could hold.

The 2026-07-26 owner ruling reworded the anchor off the word *obvious* and onto
its own operational gloss, so what is left is checkable:

> Each of the three core reading tasks — find the project state, find the next
> work, find how the parts connect — is reachable **within one tab switch from
> the landing view**, and its entry point is a **labelled** nav control or a
> **named surface on the landing view**.

## Approach

Single-module: `project-trajectory/scripts/gen_trajectory.py` +
`tests/test_gen_trajectory.py`. Bind the bar as a child `LLR` + an
`Automated: Yes` `TC` under `SR-054`, per the WI-300 option-(f) pattern — the
same shape `WI-313` used for A1/A3/A4.

**The one design constraint that makes this test worth writing.** Assert against
the **rendered artifact carrying real registry data**, not the nav skeleton.
This is the whole lesson of the 119-CRITIQUE MAJOR: the next-work surface *exists*
in the emitter (`gen_trajectory.py`, the hero `sub nowat` line, and
`test_t1_hero_names_the_active_work_item` already covers it) but rendered
**nothing** on this repo's own dashboard, because that emitter fires only on
`status == "active"` and the registry had zero active rows — a state
`test_t1_hero_active_line_absent_when_nothing_is_active` explicitly blesses. A
structural "is there a nav entry for each task" check passes on exactly the
artifact the critic failed. So:

- Derive the three tasks' entry points from the emitted document, and assert each
  resolves to a **labelled** control (a nav button with text, or a landing-view
  section with a heading) — an unlabelled region fails.
- Assert the reachability depth is **≤ 1 tab switch**: the entry point is on the
  landing view or one nav click from it, never behind a descend/expand.
- Assert the next-work surface is **non-empty across the status fixtures that
  occur in practice** — including the all-queued, zero-active registry that
  produced the failure. This is the fixture that would have bitten.
- Prove the guard bites: mutate the emitter to drop the surface (and to emit it
  unlabelled) and confirm each test reds, then restore byte-identical.

## Interfaces

*No cross-module seam — acts only within `gen_trajectory.py` and its test module.*
The frontier data it reads is already declared as `IF-071` (the `gen_trajectory`
Consumes-schedule seam); this WI adds no consumer and proposes no seam.

## Dependency — why this is gated behind WI-305

`WI-305` is the open T1 defect. Binding an anchor whose defect is still open is
the **lax classification** the WI-300 ruling names as its one failure mode: the
test would have to be written to pass against a dashboard the critique already
failed. `WI-305` fixes the surface; this WI freezes the fixed behaviour into a
check. Land them in that order, or land them together with the guard proven
against the pre-fix artifact.

## Notes / open questions

- **This does not flip `SR-054`.** After T1 binds, the live critique anchors are
  T2-legibility, T4, T5, T6, T7, T8 — `perceptual-stale` keeps firing. The
  remaining question the 2026-07-26 ruling left open is whether T8's "crossings
  minimized… in open space, not under a label" is the last genuinely perceptual
  clause under `SR-054`; T4 and T7 are browser-harness work, and the browser is
  already vendored ([`scripts/dashboard-shots/shoot.mjs`](../../../scripts/dashboard-shots/shoot.mjs)
  drives Playwright for the shot matrix), so "needs a harness" is cheaper than the
  WI-300 table assumed.
- Do **not** widen the check into "the reader can tell what to do next" — that is
  the reader-experience clause the ruling deliberately did not mechanize.
