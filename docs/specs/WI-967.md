## Problem

WI-963 (the trunk mint drops amendment records a merged range already
settled) passed its checkpoint, and its gate raised one convergence-class
MAJOR that dispute sitting 007-ADJUDICATE-1ae50f4 ruled FIX: a traced change
routed after a row's act settles silently when a later act in the range
re-copies that registry for another row. The sound remedy needs to know
which registries each act copied, and the act ledger
(`docs/archive/last_approved/acts.toml`, IF-220) does not say. The snapshot
writer appends one entry per act with only `seq`, `date`, `approved` and
`reattested`. Three act shapes copy a registry the entry never names (wi-963
D-011, probed through the real producer):

- a traced re-anchor (`intake.py snapshot --approves <registry>=<ref>` with
  no ids) copies its registry and writes `approved = []`, `reattested = []`;
- an act that mixes `--approves` on one registry with ids in another lists
  only the second;
- a re-seed copies every registry and lists only newly approved rows.

Comparing against the copy the re-attesting act's own commit wrote (the fix1
build) does not close it either: every hand landing is a `git merge
--squash`, whose one commit is the act's commit.

## Scope

Each act-ledger entry records the registries its act copied, for every act
shape the snapshot writer produces: the entry's shape (IF-220), its one
writer, and its validators, with one RESYNC entry for adopters' ledgers.
WI-963's settlement rule is **not** in scope; WI-963 rebases onto this
row's landing and builds it there.

Questions the scope critique answers before the claim:

- how an entry written before this row is read (the ledger is
  append-only and says "Do not edit");
- every reader of the ledger, found by grep (`acceptance_record`,
  `baseline_snapshot`, `gen_open_items` today), and whether the file's
  authority over acts needs a `## Trust` section (rubric S2);
- the landing order: it changes the act ledger the cycle runs on, so it
  lands alone (revision 37).

## The record (settled at the scope critique)

Each new `[[act]]` entry gains one field, `copied`: the sorted list of the
registry files the act actually copied, each a repo-relative path from the
snapshot's own registry universe (the files `SNAPSHOT_TIERS` names, the
needs file included). The writer records the set it copied, never one
reconstructed from row ids, so a traced re-anchor naming no row, a mixed
`--approves` act and a re-seed each name every file they copied. An act
that copies nothing appends no entry, as today. `seq`, `date`, `approved`,
`reattested` and `verdict` keep their meanings, and a copied file is no
evidence that its rows were judged.

**Legacy and new entries.** No existing entry is edited or backfilled. An
entry without `copied` is a legacy entry: it parses and keeps its fields,
and its copy set reads as unknown, never as empty, and is never inferred
from ids, live files or snapshot history. The boundary is the commit, not
a marker: an entry a commit adds (its `seq` absent from the parent's
ledger, as `acceptance_record._added_acts` and `risk_acceptance_act`
already read it) must carry `copied`; and in a whole-file read, no entry
may omit it after one that carries it.

## Architectural delta (within scope)

The producer (`baseline_snapshot`, CMP-006) and the IF-220 boundary are
kept; IF-220's data domain gains `copied`, its notes the legacy reading,
and its consumers list becomes the grep's. No new component, mode class
or preparatory row.

| Output | Depends on |
|---|---|
| new entry's `copied` | the files `copy_live` actually wrote in this act |
| new entry's `approved`, `reattested`, `verdict` | unchanged: the act's named rows and verdict |
| a reader's copy set for an entry | `copied` when present; unknown when absent |
| validator verdict on an entry | whether the commit adds it (against the parent), and its place after the first entry carrying `copied` |

Cases: legacy entry (no `copied`), recorded entry, and the one transition
the writer makes at its first act after this row lands. WI-963 alone adds
settlement's consumption of `copied`.

## Trust

The ledger has authority over acts: its entries back the acceptance
record's act and verdict-backing rules.

- **Producer.** `baseline_snapshot.copy_live` and `_record_act` alone write
  `docs/archive/last_approved/acts.toml`, through `intake.py snapshot`,
  run by a person or an authorized model session under the existing
  approval rules. A refusal before copying is a failed call with no act.
  An interrupted or timed-out call can leave unfinished copies or a
  partial ledger; that is no completed act, and is repaired through the
  supported act workflow before it is committed. This row adds no
  transaction or recovery mechanism (PROCESS.md §3, "When a guard is
  owed"; §6).
- **Consumers (grep).** Direct readers of the file:
  `project-trajectory/scripts/baseline_snapshot.py` (`_standing_acts`,
  `acts_problems`, `_entry_problems`, `parse_acts`, `read_acts`,
  `acts_findings`, `record_findings`, `_acts_at`, `_act_names`,
  `risk_acceptance_act`, `risk_acceptance_view`),
  `project-trajectory/scripts/acceptance_record.py` (`_ledger_acts`,
  `_added_acts`, `reattested_between`, `lane_acted_set`,
  `adjudication_backing_refusal`, `_wrote_ledger`, `SNAPSHOT_OWN_FILES`)
  and `project-trajectory/scripts/gen_open_items.py` (`_ledger_entries`,
  `verdict_reattest_block`). Entry-field consumers:
  `project-trajectory/scripts/kitlib/sitting.py` (`_named_line`,
  `unaccepted_refusal`, `uncovered_refusal`, `_ACT_KINDS`; some callers
  pass synthesized act-shaped dictionaries, which need no `copied`).
  Findings consumer: `project-trajectory/scripts/trace.py` (through
  `record_findings`). Producer route: `project-trajectory/scripts/intake.py`
  (`_cmd_snapshot`).
- **Ruling (one, shared).**
  - *No ledger:* no entries and no copy evidence; the first successful
    signing creates the file; no past act is fabricated from absence.
  - *A legacy entry:* parsed and kept byte for byte; copy set unknown;
    consumers of the old fields keep their rules; a consumer that needs
    copy evidence gets none.
  - *A new entry:* `copied` required and preserved; readers expose it
    without loss.
  - *A failed write or unparseable ledger:* refused whole, as IF-220
    states today: the snapshot act refuses before moving a byte, trace
    reports it on the integrity floor, the acceptance record's readers
    refuse an unknowable act set, `risk_acceptance_act` returns no anchor
    with its reason, and the audit block lists no entries.
  - *A registry a person edits through a supported route:* the existing
    authority checks apply, and the next successful act records what it
    copied.
  - *A hand edit of the ledger itself:* no supported route makes one (the
    file says "Do not edit"); outside the promise, and no new refusal is
    added for it.
  - **Binds to:** the entry's act (`seq`), committed with its snapshot
    copies; not the live tree, and not a judgement of every row in a
    copied file.
