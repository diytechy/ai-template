## SR-178 — Text that has moved away from its acceptance record is reported

> **Requirement.** The kit shall report any recorded artifact whose text has moved away from the
copy recording its acceptance - stakeholder needs included - regardless of any
Status movement.

> **Rationale.** THE DRIFT half, separable from SR-140 in the direction that matters: a record that exists and rides its acceptance commit, with nothing ever comparing live text against it, satisfies SR-140 completely and leaves every later amendment invisible. That is not a hypothetical - it is precisely the failure the git-history derivation had, and the reason a baseline on disk was owed at all.
REGARDLESS OF STATUS MOVEMENT is the load-bearing clause, not a qualifier. The amendment machinery watches the Status flip, and the sanctioned staged-amendment path deliberately does not flip - so a rule keyed off the flip returns nothing on exactly the rows a sitting exists to judge. Reporting against the recorded copy is the only reading that survives that, because the copy is a baseline OUTSIDE the live file and a walk over the live file can never be one.
THE STAKEHOLDER-NEED RIDER is in the normative text rather than left to the acceptance because the need tier is the one a sitting most wants to hold and the one the amendment-watching rules were last to reach: each rule walks the tiers it is given, so without the clause the need tier is left off the walk and no rule reaches it. Needs are compared row by row under the same accepted and traced split as every other tier.

## SR-207 — An acceptance act carries into the record no drift it does not itself accept

> **Requirement.** The kit shall leave, in an acceptance act, the recorded text of every row the act does not accept, re-accept or retire whose accepted text, or whose adjudicated links, differ from its live text.

> **Rationale.** An acceptance act once refreshed a registry's record by copying the whole file, so it carried every row of the file as it then stood. The kit therefore refused the act while any row it did not accept or re-accept had drifted. That kept the record to what someone saw, but it coupled every row of a file: one unblessed amendment held every blessed amendment and every first acceptance in its registry until it was ruled or reverted. Leaving each such row at its recorded text keeps the same protection by construction, since an act cannot carry text it did not name, and removes the coupling: the drifted row stays visibly drifted and owed, and no longer stops the act. Adjudicated links are the links whose change goes to an adjudicator: a requirement's needs and boundary crossings, a design row's requirements and a test case's verified rows. They are held like accepted text because the record is what tells whether that change has been settled. A row's other trace cells, which no verdict judges, and a row claiming no acceptance follow the live text whenever an act writes their file, so the record stays comparable line by line with live. The rule holds for any number of tiers in one file.


### SR SR-207
- **AcceptanceCriteria**
  - before: An acceptance act whose registry holds a row outside the act with accepted text differing from its recorded copy is refused, naming each such row and cell; adding that row to the act, as an acceptance or a re-acceptance, clears the refusal and every row it clears is named in the act's record; a registry with no such row refreshes as before; the rule holds for every tier whose rows are compared with their recorded copies, including tiers that share one registry file, so one tier's acceptance never carries another tier's drift.
  - after: An acceptance act whose registry holds a row it does not name, with accepted text or adjudicated links differing from the recorded copy, completes, records the rows it names, leaves that row's recorded text in place and names the row as owed; the same act naming that row records it from live; an unnamed accepted row whose accepted text and adjudicated links equal its recorded copy takes its live trace cells; an unnamed row claiming no acceptance takes its live text; and the rule holds for every tier compared with a recorded copy, including tiers that share one registry file, so an act on one tier carries none of another tier's drift.
