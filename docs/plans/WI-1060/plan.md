## What today does (the facts the choices rest on)

- **The copy.** `copy_live` writes each registry in the act's scope (`_authorised_registries`: a registry `--accepts` names, one holding a `--reaccepts` row, one where a row moved into acceptance) with `shutil.copyfile`: the whole live file. Registries outside the scope keep their bytes.
- **The refusal.** `refresh_refusal` refuses the act while any row of a registry in scope has drift `refresh_ledger` calls absorbed (its recorded copy claims acceptance and its accepted cells moved, or live no longer carries it) and the act does not name it. ## 1. The traced-cell rule

### How a routed traced change on an `Accepted` row is found owed today

1. **The merge mint finds it, from git, not from the record.** At the merge slot (and `intake.py sweep`), trigger (a), `intake._routed_amendments`, walks the merged range with `acceptance_record.staged_spine_amendments(before, after)`, keeps each record with an accepted change or a change in `intake.ROUTED_TRACED_CELLS` (`SN-Refs`, `Boundary-Refs` on SR; `SR-Refs` on LLR; `Verifies` on TC), and mints one `adjudication` row per merge listing each row, cell and before/after. It compares the two trunk trees; the record plays no part in finding the change.
2. **Two readers compare it with the record, both through `acceptance_record._row_settled`** (accepted cells and routed traced cells equal to the record's copy at a revision):
   - `settled_amendments` drops a minted record when an act the range added re-accepts the row and the row equals its copy at the range's end, with a later-writer arm (LLR-364). Its documented limit exists only because a later act's whole-file copy re-anchors another row's routed cell.
   - `unanchored_rows`, through `_held_act_drafts`, decides whether a row a sitting ruled MEANING on a held rung still owes the operator's act: owed while it differs from its copy in an accepted or a routed traced cell; when it reads settled, no operator row is minted.
3. **No drift reader reports it from the record.** `is_drifted`, `drifted_cells`, `owing_rows`, `refresh_ledger` and every brief read accepted cells only.

**A hole this exposes, today.** A combined sitting that re-accepts CLARITY rows of the design registry while ruling another design row MEANING on a held rung for a moved `SR-Refs` alone: the act's whole-file copy carries the MEANING row's new `SR-Refs` into the record, `unanchored_rows` then reads it settled, and no operator row is minted. The routed change reaches the record with no operator act. `adjudicate_act`'s owed/taken reading (whole parsed row equal to its copy) is fooled the same way.

### The three choices and what each does to that

| | Unnamed accepted rows' traced cells | The merge mint | `settled_amendments` | Held-act arm | Rows and tests it moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Refreshed with the act** (today's behaviour, at the row) | All refresh, routed included, on a row whose accepted cells are unchanged | unchanged | unchanged, limit stays | **the hole stays**: another act can settle a MEANING row's routed change | none beyond the whole-file rows |
| **B. Left for each row's own act** | None refresh | unchanged | limit gone | hole closed | LLR-364, TC-403; and every silent trace cell (`Evidence`, `TestRefs`, `Hat-Refs`, `Phase` ...) drifts in the record forever, since nothing ever names a row for them; the traced re-anchor act shape (TC-400) writes nothing |
| **C. Routed cells wait for their row's own act; every other traced cell refreshes** | Silent traced cells refresh; a row whose routed cell moved keeps its recorded text | unchanged | limit gone (the restored-cell case now mints, which is the right answer: the restore is a routed change from the text the act blessed) | hole closed | LLR-364, TC-403 |

**Chosen: C.** The kit already treats a routed traced cell as judged content wherever it compares a row with the record (`_row_settled`); the record should move it only where a verdict moved it. Silent traced cells are judged by nobody and stay as cheap as they are today, so the record stays diffable against live. The cost is two more accepted rows amended (LLR-364, TC-403) and the routed table moving down to where the writer and the judges can read it (section 7).

### The rule, as written into Done-when items 1 to 3

A row the act does not name **follows live** (takes its live text whole, or leaves the record when live no longer carries it) when

- its live text claims no acceptance (or, gone from live, its recorded copy claimed none), or
- its recorded copy and its live text both claim acceptance and neither its accepted cells nor its routed traced cells differ, which refreshes its other traced cells.

Every other row the act does not name keeps its recorded text, or stays out of the record if it has none. The first bullet is section 2's rule for rows claiming no acceptance; it goes into the Done-when with the traced-cell rule because item 1 as filed ("every other row ... keeps its recorded text, apart from what the traced-cell rule refreshes") would otherwise forbid a `Drafted` row following live. The parenthetical in item 3 that pointed at the plan is removed.

## 2. How rows leave and enter the record

Classification, per row of a file the act writes (the act names a row when the act ledger entry lists it as accepted or re-accepted; the writer computes `accepted` exactly as today, from live against the prior record):

| Row | Named by the act | Not named |
|---|---|---|
| In live, live claims no acceptance (`Drafted`, no `Status`, or de-accepted) | live text | live text (follows live) |
| In live, live and copy both claim acceptance, accepted and routed cells equal | live text | live text (traced refresh) |
| In live, live and copy both claim acceptance, accepted or routed cells differ | live text (re-acceptance) | **recorded text, owed** |
| In live and claiming acceptance, copy below acceptance or absent | live text (first acceptance) | recorded state (absent, or the copy below acceptance): the unanchored rule reports it |
| Gone from live, copy claims acceptance | **retired**: leaves the record | **stays, owed** (a removal no act retired) |
| Gone from live, copy claims none | leaves | leaves (follows live) |

- **A retired row leaves the record** only when the act names it, by the existing route (`--reaccepts` naming a row live no longer carries; `_refuse_reaccepts` already accepts a recorded id). Its bytes leave with it, leading comments included.
- **A first acceptance enters** at its live position with its live bytes: a row flipped into acceptance, or born accepted, is in the act's `accepted` list.
- **A `Drafted` row the act does not name follows live: today's carriage continues.** Its text in the record blesses nothing (every drift reader and the refusal ledger skip a copy below acceptance; the unanchored rule fires on a live claim whose copy reads below it), and keeping it current keeps the record diffable and keeps `adjudicate_act`'s stated reading true ("a whole-registry copy carries Drafted rows"). The alternative, `Drafted` rows entering only when named, would leave every new draft as a permanent record-versus-live difference. A row born unnamed and claiming acceptance cannot come from the writer (it always names such a row), and is kept out so the unanchored rule reports a forgery.
- **A de-accepted row follows live** (its copy becomes `Drafted`), with or without changed text. Today a de-acceptance with changed text refuses the act. Following live is safe: the copy then claims nothing, and putting the row back to `Accepted` is a first acceptance the writer names and the merge slot backs with a verdict. Keeping the old accepted text instead would make the later re-flip invisible to the writer (copy and live both `Accepted`), so the first-acceptance act would silently leave the row drifted. Decision D2 below.

## 3. Row order and every byte between rows

A registry copy is split into a head, rows and a tail:

- **Head:** the bytes before the first row's leading comments, i.e. the file's preamble. **Tail:** the bytes after the last row's last cell line.
- **A row** is its leading run of blank and whole-line comment lines (after the previous row's last cell line), its table header `[<tier table>.<id>]` (the id may be quoted), and its cells up to its last non-blank, non-comment line, comments inside the row included (as `SN-023` has). Line terminators belong to the line they end.

The result of an act on a file:

- **head and tail are live's** (non-row prose rides every act, as today);
- **rows are in live order**, each row's bytes taken whole from the side the classification names (named or following: live's; kept: the record's);
- **a kept row live no longer carries** (an unretired removal) is placed after the row it followed in the previous copy, or first if none preceded it;
- **no newline translation**: bytes are read and written as bytes. A copy whose rows all came from live (every act that keeps no row) is the live file byte for byte, so a record stays a plain `git diff` away from live and differs from it only inside kept rows.

The split is validated, fail-closed: the head, rows and tail must concatenate to the original text; each row must parse alone (header plus body) to exactly the row the whole file's parse holds under that id; and every row table the file's parse holds must be a split row. A text that fails (a header-looking line inside a multi-line string, a non-row table, a dotted key) refuses the act before anything is written. No registry the kit writes has any of these (checked: the eight snapshotted registries and their templates hold only row tables).

## 4. Tiers that share one file

`external.toml` (entities, crossings, relationships), `assumptions.toml` (assumptions, surrogates) and the needs file (needs, stakeholders) are each one copy. Rows are keyed by (table, id), classified under their own tier's cell split, and ordered as live orders them across tiers; one head and one tail per file. Named ids map to their tier by prefix, as `_reaccepted_registry` does. Since a kept row never refuses the act, one tier's drift no longer touches another tier's act at all.

## 5. The off-spine registries (interfaces, components, external)

The same row rule, with no special case. Their scope stays as it is (written only when the act names a row there, a row there moves into acceptance, or `--accepts` names the registry), they stay outside the acceptance-act rung at the merge slot (`OUTSIDE_THE_ACCEPTANCE_ACT`, OI-30 D3), and no traced cell of theirs is routed (routing is spine-only), so an unnamed accepted off-spine row follows live whenever its accepted cells are unchanged. What changes is that an operator act on one off-spine row no longer has to name every other drifted row of that registry.

## 6. The seed, the re-seed and the repair

- **The first (seed) snapshot is unchanged:** the directory does not exist, the act needs `--seed`, it writes every registry whole, and its ledger entry lists every claiming row as accepted and `kept = []`. Under the judge it reads clean by construction (section 7: with no previous copy and every claiming row named, the expected copy is the live file).
- **A re-seed over a standing record** (`--seed` with the directory present, which `copy_live` already treats as a refresh of every registry) becomes a row-scoped act over every registry, as any act is. Today it refuses while any row drifted; now it keeps the drifted rows. It still takes no `--reaccepts` and writes no stamp (it is still a first signing for those purposes). Decision D6.
- **The repair** (a record copy that does not parse) still copies whole files: the writer cannot split a copy it cannot read, its prior record reads as none, so every claiming row is in `accepted`, and the judge treats a previous copy that does not parse as no copy, giving the same expected copy. The bound stays the one stated today: corrupting the record first means committing the corruption, which the hook refuses.

## 7. The construction

### Where the code goes, and why there

`baseline_snapshot.py` measures 980 SLOC and `acceptance_record.py` 972 against the module-size ratchet's threshold of 1000, so neither can take the new code. A new module, **`project-trajectory/scripts/record_rows.py`** (header `Component: CMP-006`, stdlib plus `spine_carrier`, imported one way by both), holds the row model and the rule. It imports neither the writer nor the judge: the caller passes in the cell comparison (`acceptance_record.split_changed_cells`), so no import edge or cycle is added. The two mirror rules stay in `acceptance_record.py` under their names, so `check_trajectory`'s re-exports, `test_acceptance_record.py`'s census of moved names and WI-1059's one-line rename there are untouched, and nothing moves that WI-1045 is to move. If either existing module still crosses 1000 SLOC, the git-reading helper moves to `record_rows.py` rather than a baseline being bumped.

### `record_rows.py` (new)

- `ROUTED_TRACED_CELLS`: moved here from `intake.py` (under choice C both the writer and the judges need it, and neither may import `intake`). `intake.ROUTED_TRACED_CELLS` stays as an alias, so the mint and `settled_amendments`/`unanchored_rows` callers are unchanged.
- `RowRecordError(ValueError)`: what the split and the composition raise; the writer turns it into a refusal, the judge into a finding.
- `row_segments(text, rel)`: the split of section 3, validated.
- `row_rule(rel, prev_rows, live_rows, named, split)`: the one home of sections 1 and 2's classification, `{(table, id): "live" | "record" | None}` plus the kept ids.
- `expected_copy(rel, prev_text, live_text, named, split)`: `(text, kept)`. When every row is named or follows live it returns the live text unchanged (no split needed, so the seed, the repair, every act that keeps nothing and every legacy-carrier file take this path). Otherwise it splits both sides and composes per section 3; on a legacy carrier (CSV, or the needs file's markdown) on either side it raises, naming the rows that would be kept.
- `copy_problems(rel, prev_text, live_text, written_text, named, split)`: `[]` when `written_text` is what the act writes; otherwise one line per row (an unnamed row's text carried in, a named row not equal to live, a row dropped that no act retired, a row present that the rule keeps out) read by parsing `written_text` and comparing each row with the side the rule names, independent of the split, and then one line for bytes outside every row when the rows agree but the bytes do not.
