## Views for the owner

The parent-LLR link moves component-boundary obligations from the `DevStg-Reqs` rung, which recurses per component scope today and whose boundary a human signs in the shipped default, to the LLR rung the adjudicator approves on a released rung. Here `human_approval_through = "DevStg-Boundary"` already releases both, so nothing changes in this repo; in an adopter holding every tier, an obligation that used to be the owner's signature as a sub-scope SR becomes an adjudicator's act as a child LLR. The plan names the ladder text, orphan rules and migrations together, which is right; this is only to say the authority consequence belongs in that same sentence.

D-004 is landed code, not a pending design (finding 7). The design held under the breaks I looked for, so the useful review of it now is the one the queue already runs: the lane's reviews when WI-798 and WI-800 touch the store. I would not spend a further session on it.

The headless-coordinator ruling (finding 3) is the one whose cost is easy to under-read: the marker is one line in `overnight_legs.py`, but the kit's hook and merge slot then treat the leg exactly as they treat the managed loop, and the coordinator's lanes do not behave like the loop's (they span sessions and modes). If the aim is only that a headless leg never takes a held act, the leg prompt already forbids it and the held-status rung needs the marker; the trailer floor on every commit is the part that binds lanes to a mode. Worth deciding with that trade in view before B1 writes the sitting briefs.
