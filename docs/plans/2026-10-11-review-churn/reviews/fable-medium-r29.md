- [MAJOR] [phase 2] README.md:1077 (item 16, step 2) -> The claim "the lane writes the reserved marks into its own watermark as values ... so the integrity rule (no live id above its mark) holds" names only rules 1–2 of the watermark. `trace.py` also enforces rules 3–4 (`_mark_history_findings`, trace.py:1210–1278; pinned by `tests/test_id_watermark.py::test_a_hand_raised_mark_is_a_finding`): a committed mark may not rise past `max(previous committed mark, highest live id)`, and the only exception is a one-shot `--correct-mark` record citing a ruling. A reservation is exactly a raise with no live row behind it, on the carrier (which holds no new rows at all) and in the lane before it drafts, so both fail `--strict-integrity` as designed today. Two more facts the step assumes away: `--bump-ids` cannot "become the reserve step" because `bump_watermark` (trace.py:1508) only raises each mark to `live_max_ids`, it takes no count; and the station refresh refuses any conflict on `docs/id-watermark` (`integrate.py` `_resolve_generated_conflicts` auto-resolves `[generated]` paths only, and the watermark is deliberately not one, integrate.py:230–239), so "each space takes the higher mark" at landing is new resolution code, not the file's own behaviour (`committed_watermark`'s max-over-parents only sets the monotonicity *baseline*, trace.py:1473). -> State in item 16 that rule 4's justification changes from "allocation history" to "the carrier's committed mark" (the hand-raise guard is re-founded, not kept as is), that a reserve verb taking a count per space replaces `--bump-ids` as the minter, and that the refresh/landing resolves the watermark per space to the max. Without this the next build either violates an integrity check or quietly retires it.

## Views for the owner

**Item 14 as it now stands.** The direction is right and the scope is now the right size. Bounding the hats to the SN → SR step matches how the surface is computed today: `hat_surface_for_work_item` (plan_briefs.py:359) selects hats from the roster's `applies_when` evaluated against the WI and each parent need it reaches through `SR-Refs` → `SN-Refs` or a canonical `SpecRef`, so a round that adds SRs under a need already has the context it needs, and LLR/TC rounds would owe the ten `always` outcomes for nothing. The retirement list is complete on the surfaces I could find (`PROCESS.md:87`, `spine-questions.md:172–175`, `hats.template.toml:80`, `hats.toml:57–61`, `RESYNC_PACK.md:6753`, `hats.py` from line 855, `tests/test_hats_record.py`, LLR-297, TC-312, LLR-183's clause, TC-211's `inputs`, IF-133's `load`, `components.derived.toml`); the one gap is SR-161's title (MINOR above). SAFETY leaving the roster costs nothing here (no row cites it) and is the kind of values divergence the dogfood rule allows. The one trap is not in the plan's text but under it: the local `contract_split` ref is the pre-ruling ancestor, so "match `contract_split`" must be pinned to `961f9e47` before anyone ports a row.

**Item 15.** Right, and cheap: the batch is the only multi-WI path left (`dispatch.py` admit/exclusive, `integrate.py`'s `;`-joined claim and its per-spec move pairs, `handback.py`'s per-row close, `agent_loop.py`'s combined-diff review scope), and the coordinator path already runs one WI per lane. The port list is the divergence report's groups 1 and 4 plus the consequential LLRs and their TCs, with TC-146 and TC-205 correctly split; it misses LLR-061/TC-061, which are the loop-side refusal the step promises. "Spine exclusivity stays as it is until item 16" is consistent with what the dispatcher does once the batch is gone (a spine WI still admits alone into an idle station), so nothing is left ambiguous between the two items.

**Item 16: the carrier, the keep-or-mint rule, and the adjudicator without the latch.** The carrier is the lightest design that meets the owner's requirement that every mint has one writer; I could not name a simpler one that does not either wait on the station lock (reserve on trunk) or keep the hand-briefed floor (today). Its cost is real but small: trunk's own mints (`intake.py next_wi_id` reads the WI space of the same file, so WI ids already live where the plan puts them) gain a lock step, and three existing rules must be re-founded rather than left alone (the hand-raise guard, `--bump-ids`' live-max semantics, the ledger's file-order rule). The plan presents the carrier as compatible with those rules, and it is not; that is the one thing the build must know. The keep-or-mint rule is a judgement the sitting applies and needs no machinery; it should replace F point 6 outright rather than sit beside it. On the adjudicator: the owner's instinct is sound for the *judging* half, and the plan's snapshot reading is correct, but the latch also keeps acts on rebased text. The answer that over-builds nothing is to let the verdict move between lanes and keep the act (Status flip plus whole-file snapshot) in landing order; a per-row snapshot or a re-taken copy would be machinery against a corner that ordering already closes.

**The consolidation, and what else to weigh.** The Status, the summary table, the rulings section, phase 0 and `docs/status.md`'s resume line agree on what is ruled (1–10, decision 4, 14–16), what is the owner's position (F, 13), what is open and what is for a closer look; the handoff's "For the owner" block ends on the same state; revision 28 moved 285 lines out of the plan and I found nothing a later reader needs that left with them (the dispositions on item 14's slot optionality and SR-161's `coincident` are still reflected in the item). Two things to weigh beyond the findings. First, the `contract_split` ref: whether to reset the local branch to origin or keep it and pin commits everywhere; either is fine, but one must happen before item 14 or 15 is built. Second, the plan now carries three id-rule statements across items 11 and 16 and `pointer-rules.md`; the consolidation would be complete with one.
