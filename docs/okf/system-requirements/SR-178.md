# SR-178 — Text that has moved away from its acceptance record is reported

**Requirement.** The kit shall report any recorded artifact whose text has moved away from the
copy recording its acceptance - stakeholder needs included, despite their
carrying no status cell - regardless of any Status movement.

**Acceptance criteria.** An artifact whose normative text has moved since the copy that recorded its acceptance is reported whether or not its Status moved; a ratified cell moving under a row still claiming approval is reported, a merely traced cell moving is not, and a row below approval has no claim to fall from and is never reported; the status marker itself is never read as the movement, so a flip is neither an amendment nor a mask for one; and a stakeholder need is held to the same rule despite carrying no status cell for the amendment machinery to watch.

**Rationale.** MINTED 2026-08-18 by the SR-140 split (one-decision doctrine R1). This is the DRIFT half, and it is separable from SR-140 in the direction that matters: a record that exists and rides its approval commit, with nothing ever comparing live text against it, satisfies SR-140 completely and leaves every later amendment invisible. That is not a hypothetical - it is precisely the failure the git-history derivation had, and the reason a baseline on disk was owed at all.
REGARDLESS OF STATUS MOVEMENT is the load-bearing clause, not a qualifier. The amendment machinery watches the Status flip, and the sanctioned staged-amendment path deliberately does not flip - so a rule keyed off the flip returns nothing on exactly the rows a sitting exists to judge. Reporting against the recorded copy is the only reading that survives that, because the copy is a baseline OUTSIDE the live file and a walk over the live file can never be one.
THE STAKEHOLDER-NEED RIDER is in the normative text rather than left to the acceptance because a need has no Status cell at all: every amendment-watching rule in the kit is keyed off a field the need tier does not have, so without the clause the tier a sitting most wants to hold is the one tier the rule cannot reach. Needs are covered by the whole-file copy rather than by a per-need projection - the three need tables have different shapes, and a projection would have recorded the garbling.
WHAT COUNTS AS MOVEMENT is one definition with one home, shared with the amend-without-flip guard: the id column and the Status cell are excluded STRUCTURALLY (the id is the join key rather than content, and folding Status in would make every flip read as an amendment and every amendment invisible behind its own flip), and the remainder splits into a ratified half and a traced half. Two readers of one basis rather than two rules that agree until they do not.
