# LLR-115 — Task findability - labelled entry within one tab switch (T1 core)

**Detail.** The mechanized core of usability anchor T1 (docs/rubrics/dashboard-usability.md). Each of the three core reading tasks the rubric concretizes — find the project state, find the next work, find how the parts connect — is reachable within one tab switch of the landing view, its entry point a LABELLED nav control or a NAMED surface on the landing view, never an unlabelled region a reader must discover by clicking. THE DESIGN CONSTRAINT that makes the check honest: it is judged against the RENDERED artifact carrying real registry data, NEVER the nav skeleton. The guard resolves each task's entry point from the emitted document (task 1 -> the landing Definition-completeness/Execution cards, 0 switches; task 2 -> the landing Next-work card, 0 switches; task 3 -> the How-SW nav tab, 1 switch), asserts each is LABELLED and within one switch, and asserts the next-work surface is NON-EMPTY on the zero-active/all-queued fixture. SCOPE — PROVEN: a labelled entry point per task, reachable in one switch, and the next-work surface non-empty across the practice fixtures (zero-active, waiting-with-predecessor, drained). DELIBERATELY NOT: whether the reader subjectively knows what to do next; T1 leaves the critique entirely, with no residue under LLR-055/TC-055.

**Rationale.** 'Obvious' was always glossed as labelled-rather-than-unlabelled, and that gloss is the whole bar — once it is written operationally there is nothing perceptual left to adjudicate, so the anchor can leave the critique instead of being re-judged every round. The rendered-artifact constraint is the load-bearing part and must not be relaxed to a structural check: a next-work surface can EXIST in the emitter and render nothing when no row is active, so a nav-skeleton check passes on exactly the artifact a reader finds empty.
