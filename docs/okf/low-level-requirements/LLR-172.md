# LLR-172 — Component derivation record: element completeness + the ranking recompute

**Detail.** Extends the existing component pass — the one home that already reads docs/requirements/components.toml and already emits CMP findings — with the derivation-record rules SR-165 states, rather than adding a second reader of the same registry. Per live CMP row: the record must declare the candidate partitions considered, the objective and the constraints they were scored against, a score per candidate, the selected partition, and the human ruling that named the components and adopted it; a missing element is one finding naming the row AND the element. Two derived rules on top of presence, because presence alone is satisfiable by a record that says nothing: (1) THE RANKING RECOMPUTES — re-rank the recorded candidates from the record's own objective, constraints and scores and compare with the recorded order, a mismatch being a finding, which is what makes the record falsifiable without a reviewer re-running a model session; (2) A SELECTION THE SCORES DO NOT RANK FIRST is a finding unless the record carries a human override naming that choice, so a legitimate "the scores said A, the human chose B" is expressible and an unexplained one is not. Vacuous by construction on a project declaring no components, and on the example -000 rows. NOT BUILT YET: the record cells do not exist on the CMP schema today, which is why this row and TC-166 are Draft — SR-165 was flipped Inspection -> Test at act 7 precisely because the check is mechanizable, and this is the debt that flip incurred, recorded rather than silently owed.

**Rationale.** The check belongs where component_findings already is for the reason the census keeps applying: a second module reading components.toml would be a second answer to "is this partition recorded", and the two would drift. The recompute is the load-bearing half — an element-presence check passes on a record whose scores contradict its own conclusion, which is the exact failure SN-040 exists to catch (the next reviser cannot tell load-bearing boundaries from accidents). The override arm is what stops the recompute rule from forbidding human judgement: the requirement is that a departure from the scores is RECORDED, never that it is disallowed.
