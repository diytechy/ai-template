# LLR-113 — No information by colour alone (A3 core)

**Detail.** The mechanized core of accessibility anchor A3: for every declared colour vocabulary (enumerated from the module constants, the U5/U2 closure, so a new vocabulary enters by existing), every member that actually PAINTS in a rendered document resolves to a legend swatch labelled in words in that same document, with CSS tokens resolved. Status members additionally pair a per-status glyph. The emitted JS detail-badge maps are SUBSTITUTED from TIER_FILL/STATUS_FILL rather than hand-copied, and three drift guards hold every copy equal to the constants: the emitted JS maps byte-match the Python palettes; the CSS status tokens match STATUS_FILL and are asserted theme-invariant; and the tier-legend bijection derives its expectation from the TIER_FILL dict, so a member dropped from the legend also fails. SCOPE and its narrowing: the worded cue must exist in the same document — that it sits within eyeshot of the painted element is layout this does not re-assert. The JS detail-panel badge stays out of the LEGEND rule because its background carries its concept as the badge's own visible text.

**Rationale.** Colour alone excludes any reader who cannot resolve it, and the failure is silent: the page looks informative to everyone who can. A raw hex scan was rejected as the detection method because tokenised fills under-report it — the status legend paints through a CSS variable, and a hex scan finds one member of four. The substitution and the three guards exist because a hand-copied palette copy is the recurring defect here: one stale hex shipped in three separate places, and each copy that a test shares with its emitter is a copy the test cannot catch. The same-document narrowing costs a reader nothing today (every painted member is cued in its own tab panel) and a re-attestation should bless it knowingly.
