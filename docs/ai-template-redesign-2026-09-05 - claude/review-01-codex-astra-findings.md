**Details and corrections**

**1. Preserve the approval distinction.** SN-029 explicitly requires an amended requirement to lower the derived stage as a newly introduced requirement does. D-9 separates a row’s approved text from demonstrated chain completeness; it does not exempt LLR meaning changes. With human approval through the LLR tier, a stage clamp could allow automation past an unresolved human-held amendment. Correct the plan to exempt non-normative relocations through the existing approved/traced-cell distinction, while retaining approval for normative changes; otherwise explicitly amend SN-029 and its authority mapping.

**2. Define an implementable identity protocol.** Let closed work be commit `C`; recording its review creates `R`, whose tip is no longer `C`. Rebasing after review introduces another mismatch even if verdict records move elsewhere. Specify a reviewed parent or content digest, authenticated evidence outside that identity, and a policy for trunk movement; refresh before the final review if the composed result must be reviewed. Close-before-review can remove the mechanical-close exception, but cannot remove all identity treatment.

**3. Separate noisy measurement from accepting regression.** An overloaded run could raise the wall-clock ceiling, after which a real slowdown passes beneath the inflated value. Applying the same rule to deterministic complexity or coverage can ratify changed instrumentation, missing execution or a bad baseline without investigating it. Keep acceptance explicit and bind measurements to tool versions, test selection and environment; use controlled repeated timing measurements for noise. Remove “a routine change reds at most one ratchet”: one change can legitimately violate several independent guarantees.
