WHAT THE NUMBERS CHANGE ABOUT THE QUESTION, stated plainly because it is easy to read them as merely disappointing. THREE THINGS.

FIRST, the CLI family was the outlier the (d) skeptic said it was. Restatement falls 87.7% to 64.3% and the share of rows carrying something real rises from two in five to two in three. Only 21 of 108 rows are pure restatement end to end, where WI-512 found 16 of 27. Relocation still has a real population to move - 8,975 characters over 71 rows - but it is no longer a cleanup that happens to leave a residue; the residue IS most of the rows.

SECOND, the two families are genuinely different and a single ruling covering both is a ruling made on an average. Provides rows are longer and denser in remainder but nearly free of non-crossing prose (2.9%); Consumes rows carry 19.0% non-crossing. A ruling could reasonably differ per family.

THIRD - and this is the finding that reshapes the question rather than just answering it - the measurement found a class the relocation framing has no destination for. 15.3% of characters are NON-CROSSING: neither recoverable from another home nor a fact about the crossing. Five rows (IF-082/083/084/085/138) state in their own text that their contract is another row's and then spend 110-250 characters explaining why the row exists; IF-112 spends 330 of 468 characters on the defect narrative of the carrier it replaced. A contract header would inherit that content unchanged, because it is not about the code either. The kit already ships the right home and uses it ONCE in 135 rows: the rationale field (IF-141). An independent mechanism agrees on the population - trace.py's Contract-argues advisory fires on 27 of these rows and its over-ceiling advisory on 30.

A FOURTH FACT THE RELOCATION SIDE MUST ANSWER: consumer-side obligations. The measurement had to add a remainder category for a rule that binds the READER - IF-115's *"a row that DECLARES a brief this seam cannot compose must be held for a human, never dispatched with the worker assignment"*, whose own notes say the obligation *"would otherwise be recorded nowhere"*. IF-124, IF-050 and IF-116 carry the same shape. A provider-side contract header structurally cannot state it: the provider is not the party bound. Any ruling for relocation must say where this class goes, or it strands it.

AND A FIFTH: the relocation TARGET is less built than OI-62's brief assumed. The CLI relocation worked because gen_arch_map.py --cli-doc produces docs/cli-reference.md - a COMMITTED artifact with a check.py freshness step and a docs/stack.ini [generated] row. The module symbol inventory has no equivalent: the committed module map RETIRED at WI-455 and nothing succeeded it. gen_arch_map.py does harvest each public symbol's name, signature and one-line docstring on demand, so the derivation exists; what does not exist is a committed, freshness-gated reference for a registry cell to point AT. Adopting a contract-header convention therefore means building that artifact and its gate, not only agreeing a docstring line.

Blast radius

(a) STAY IN THE CELL is the zero-radius answer and leaves the registry as it is; every other option crosses the kit/instance line, which is the whole reason OI-62 held this question back for its own ruling. A contract-header convention lives in the SHIPPED kit: PROCESS.md section 8's field list, the interfaces.template.toml header, INTERFACES.template.md, gen_arch_map.py's harvester, check_trajectory.py's both-directions citation check, a new generated reference plus its check.py freshness step and its docs/stack.ini [generated] row, tests/test_dogfood_sync.py parity between the kit template and this repo's instance, and a RESYNC_PACK.md entry on the OI-12/OI-13 precedent - EVERY ADOPTING REPO inherits it, so it is a kit version bump.

Sizing the authoring honestly, from the measurement rather than from an estimate: 8,975 characters of remainder over 71 rows would move to headers on the 57-of-76 modules that already carry a Contracts: line (the 19 that do not would need one first); 28,305 characters of restatement would be deleted wherever the pass runs; and 6,715 characters of non-crossing content would need a decision of its own - rationale, the log, or deletion - under EVERY option including (a), since staying in the cell does not make a changelog a contract.

The asymmetry OI-62 ruled (e) to avoid is now measurable and it has GROWN: (e) was preferred over (b) because an editing pass would author the remainder into a cell that relocation would lift out again, and that double-authoring was reckoned against a 12.3% remainder. It is now 20.4% - the double-authoring cost is two-thirds larger than the estimate the (e) ruling was made on, which strengthens rather than weakens the case for settling placement BEFORE the editing pass runs. What it also means is that (b)'s deferred editing pass is a bigger job than its own brief assumed.

The cost of NOT ruling: the 108 rows keep 28,305 characters of restatement and their rot exposure. That exposure is now demonstrated twice, not once - IF-055 (SCHED_*, caught by the (d) tripwire) and IF-117 (three false claims: a present-tense reference to the module map retired at WI-455, a false claim about which oracle the sym: tier reads, and a stale count of 149 LLR rows against a live 184), which the tripwire structurally cannot reach.

Options
  • (a) STAY IN THE CELL. Rule that contract remains the home for the irreducible remainder, and let the deferred editing pass (OI-62's (b), still unrun) delete the restatement in place. FOR: zero kit radius, no adopter migration, no new generated artifact and no new freshness gate; the measured remainder rate of 65.7% of rows says the cell is carrying real content on most rows rather than being a near-empty formality, which is an argument for leaving it where the registry's own readers already look; and it is the only option executable by one pass over this repo alone. AGAINST: it settles by default the question the owner raised - a cell held to the code by nobody keeps being held to the code by nobody, and the two rot exhibits (IF-055, IF-117) are what that costs; it also leaves the 12 requirement-owned Provides rows with a remainder and no design row to carry it.
  • (b) RELOCATE THE REMAINDER TO A COMPONENT-SIDE CONTRACT HEADER, cell keeps the crossing and points. The Contracts: IF-### docstring line grows a structured body; gen_arch_map.py harvests it into a new committed generated reference; check.py freshness-gates that reference; the cell states the crossing and stops. FOR: the declaration sits beside the code that must honour it, which is the one change that makes the rot class self-correcting rather than checkable-after-the-fact - the WI-512 CLI relocation is the worked precedent and it held; it is the doctrinally consistent answer (decompose-don't-paraphrase, generated-not-hand-maintained); and 57 of 76 modules already carry the anchor. AGAINST: the largest radius in this brief and every adopter inherits it; it needs a NEW generated artifact and gate built, which OI-62's brief did not price; it has no destination for the consumer-side obligation class or for the 15.3% non-crossing; and on the 21 rows that are pure restatement it moves nothing, so the benefit concentrates on 71 rows.
  • (c) SPLIT BY FAMILY. Relocate on Provides (21.2% remainder, 2.9% non-crossing, and the provider IS the module) and rule stay-in-cell on Consumes (where the far side is often a file or an external party with no module to hold a header). FOR: it is the answer the per-family numbers actually support, and it dissolves the consumer-side-obligation objection by construction - a Consumes row's obligation stays where the consumer's registry row is; the Provides population is 19 rows, a single reviewable pass. AGAINST: two rules where the kit prefers one; an adopter must learn which family they are in before they know where to write; and the Provides/Consumes distinction is precisely the column WI-455 SHED, so ruling on it re-introduces as a convention the thing that was deleted as a field.
  • (d) RULE THE NON-CROSSING QUESTION FIRST AND DEFER PLACEMENT. Take no position on relocation; rule instead that non-crossing content leaves contract for rationale or the log, and re-ask placement once the cells hold only crossing facts. FOR: it is the cheapest real improvement available - 6,715 characters, 15.3%, needing no kit change at all since rationale already ships and is already used (IF-141); it makes the placement question easier to answer honestly, because the cells being compared would then contain only the content the question is about; and trace.py's existing Contract-argues advisory already names 27 of the rows, so the work is partly enumerated. AGAINST: it is a second deferral of the owner's actual question, and OI-62's own text warns that *"a measured number with no row to rule on is how a deferred question becomes a forgotten one"*; it also banks no cleanup of the 64.3% restatement, which is the bulk.
  • (e) RELOCATE ONLY THE FAIL-LOUD AND WRITTEN-ARTIFACT CLASSES (categories A and B of the verdict document's taxonomy), leaving typed shapes, vocabularies, postures and consumer obligations in the cell. FOR: those two classes are the ones a module can actually assert about itself and the ones whose staleness is most dangerous (a refusal that no longer refuses); it is a much smaller kit change than (b) and needs no new generated artifact if the header stays prose. AGAINST: a per-clause rule is harder to teach and to check than a per-row one, and an adopter deciding clause by clause will decide inconsistently; it also leaves the cell carrying most of what it carries today, so the measured benefit is small.
Recommendation

Driver's reading, and it moved during the measurement rather than being written to fit it.

Going in, the expectation was that a high restatement rate would make (b) close to obvious. It did not survive the population: 64.3% is not 87.7%, two in three rows carry something real, and the pilot family turns out to have been chosen for properties (a uniform shape, one owning boundary, and a generated argparse home that already existed) that the other 108 rows do not share. A recommendation that carried the pilot's conclusion across would be exactly the extrapolation OI-62's own brief warned against.

RECOMMEND (d) THEN RE-ASK, with (c) as the strong second. The reasoning, in the order it matters:

The non-crossing finding is the one that should move a ruler, because it is the only part of this brief where the right action is unambiguous and cheap. 15.3% of these cells is content about the REGISTRY - why a row exists, how it differs from its sibling, what defect prompted it - and no placement ruling improves it, because it is not about the code and not about the crossing. It has a home already (rationale, shipped, used once), needs no kit change, no adopter migration and no new gate, and the kit's own advisory already enumerates most of the population. Doing it first is not a delay tactic: it is the only way to ask the placement question about the content the question is actually about. Compare IF-112 and IF-107, both around 470 characters: one is a bug story and one is a type-coercion fact, and any per-row average over that pair is meaningless.

Against (b) directly: it is the doctrinally attractive answer and the one the owner's raise pointed at, so declining it needs a reason rather than a hesitation. The reason is that two of its preconditions are unmet and neither is small. It has no destination for consumer-side obligations - a class the measurement had to invent a category for, and which four rows carry as their WHOLE remainder - and it has no committed generated reference to point at, because the module map retired at WI-455 and nothing replaced it. Ruling (b) today rules for a mechanism that does not exist yet, on a population whose restatement rate is a quarter lower than the number that made it attractive. That is the arriving-by-default failure in the other direction.

Against (a): it is the honest zero-radius answer and a ruler who weighs adopter cost heavily should take it. What it costs is the thing the owner raised in the first place - the cell stays held to the code by nobody, and IF-117 is what that produces when nobody is looking. If (a) is ruled, it should be ruled WITH the non-crossing cleanup, not instead of it.

(c) is the answer the numbers most directly support and the driver would not argue against a ruler who took it: relocate on Provides where the provider is a module, stay in the cell on Consumes where the far side is a file or an external party. Its real cost is doctrinal rather than practical - it re-introduces as a convention the Provides/Consumes distinction WI-455 deleted as a column, and this pass had to recover that distinction from git history to run its own tranches, which is itself evidence that the split is still load-bearing whether or not a column carries it. That is worth the owner knowing before ruling either way.

(e) is not recommended: a per-clause rule is the hardest thing to teach an adopter and the easiest to apply inconsistently, and it buys the least.

WHAT THE DRIVER IS NOT CLAIMING. The character counts for remainder and non-crossing are spans of the clauses judged, not post-edit lengths - a pass forbidden to write cannot measure by subtraction the way WI-512 did. They are accurate to the clause, not to the character. The ROW counts (108 read, 71 carrying a remainder, 21 pure restatement, 16 carrying zero remainder at non-trivial length) are exact, and the recommendation rests on those rather than on the percentages.
