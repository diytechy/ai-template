# Change-review record quality

This rubric derives from SN-024's independent subjective acceptance,
SN-037's coordinated counterpart changes, and SN-012's proportionate
decomposition. SR-184–SR-186 make those outcomes inspectable. It is a rubric
for the delivered guidance and a scoped change record, not a replacement
for their tests or an approval of their Drafted text. A review names the Git
revision or content digest of this rubric that it used.

- **G1 — An independent decision is reconstructible.** A reader can identify
  the fresh non-author reviewer, the written rubric and its SN/SR intent
  sources, and the numbered anchors supporting findings and approval. An
  attended reviewer is human-chosen; unattended family policy remains with
  the approved routing contract. Metadata the reviewer cannot know is supplied
  by the existing invocation record, never invented.
- **G2 — The rubric can expose a permissive test.** Guidance requires deriving
  the bar from stakeholder/requirement intent and challenging a rubric copied
  from a lax TC. A populated provenance field alone cannot make that copied
  bar adequate.
- **G3 — A counterpart decision preserves meaning.** A scoped change names the
  changed requirement/interface side and its affected counterpart, with the
  corresponding change or a reason to retain it. An independent reviewer
  judges that reason; resolving two IDs alone cannot establish agreement.
- **G4 — Each child earns its review cost.** The scoped decomposition identifies
  each retained child's independent decision or verification purpose and why
  further splitting stops. Required tiers and real verification remain;
  paraphrases, mirrored tests and a row-count target do not earn extra rows.
- **G5 — A contributor can follow the instructions as delivered.** The process
  and actual prompts point to usable existing records, preserve attended and
  unattended paths, and make missing evidence visible. Passing syntax checks
  cannot stand in for the semantic judgment, and a reviewer need not invent
  hidden runtime facts or adopt an additional schema to comply.

Bad anchors are the corresponding failure classes: **B1** missing or invented
review identity/provenance; **B2** TC-derived or unanchored acceptance;
**B3** a one-sided change with no defensible counterpart decision;
**B4** decomposition that adds no independent purpose or omits a required tier;
**B5** unusable or contradictory record instructions. Cite the applicable
numbered anchors even when the verdict has no findings.
